Nintendo Entertainment System (NES)

Plastic casing, stamped metal chassis
Catalog ID: NES-1983-01

This Nintendo Entertainment System, donated by a Seattle collector, helped revive home gaming in North America. Released in 1983, it introduced iconic franchises such as Super Mario Bros and The Legend of Zelda, setting the stage for decades of console innovation.

Though physically modest, with some cosmetic wear and controller port scratches, its impact on gaming culture was immense. Collectors still prize its design for its simplicity and enduring nostalgic value.

Sony PlayStation 1

Plastic casing, optical drive, circuit boards
Catalog ID: PS1-1994-01

This PlayStation 1, donated from a Tokyo private collection, helped transform video games into a global storytelling medium. Released in 1994, it popularized 3D graphics and the CD-ROM format, offering longer narratives and immersive soundtracks that set it apart from its cartridge-based rivals.

Although physically modest — a small rectangular console with only slight surface wear — its cultural role was vast. Games on the PlayStation reimagined age-old mythic patterns: heroes facing trials, worlds on the brink of collapse, and journeys that echoed oral epics. At the same time, the platform revealed tensions between industry control and community creativity. While Sony’s blockbuster titles defined the market, fan communities and modders preserved, translated, and reinterpreted its stories.

Pokémon Red Cartridge

Plastic cartridge, printed label, internal circuit board
Catalog ID: POK-1996-01

From a Tokyo collector, this Pokémon Red cartridge launched one of the most influential franchises in gaming history. Released in 1996, it introduced players to a world of creatures to collect, train, and battle, pioneering mechanics that shaped role-playing games for decades.
